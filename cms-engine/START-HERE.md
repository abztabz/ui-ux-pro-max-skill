# Start Here (plain-English guide)

This is the "CMS Engine" — the thing that lets you edit your website yourself
(text, photos, blog posts) without calling a developer.

You do **not** need to understand the code. There are only two moments you'll
ever deal with it:

---

## Moment 1: Setting it up on a new website

You don't do this by hand. **Ask an AI assistant to do it for you.** Copy-paste
this to Claude (or any coding AI):

> Set up the "CMS Engine" from the `cms-engine` folder in my
> ui-ux-pro-max-skill repo as the editor for my new website. Wire it up,
> fill in my site's details, and tell me my admin web address and how to log in.

Then it'll ask you a few plain questions (your website name, which pages you
want to edit) and handle the rest. When it's done it gives you:

- a web address for your editor (something like **yoursite.com/admin**)
- a password

That's it. Save those two things somewhere safe.

---

## Moment 2: Actually editing your site (the everyday part)

1. Go to **yoursite.com/admin** in your browser.
2. Type your **password**, click **Log in**.
3. You'll see a simple dashboard. Click around the left menu:
   - **Page text** — click any box, type your change.
   - **Blog posts** — write a post like an email, click Publish.
   - **Photos** — upload straight from your phone.
   - **SEO** — the title/description Google shows.
4. Click **Save**. Your site updates on its own in about a minute.

No saving files, no code, no publishing steps. Type, Save, done.

---

## Good to know

- **It's safe to click around.** Nothing goes live until you hit Save.
- **If a Save shows a red message, just read it** — it's written in plain
  English and tells you what to fix (e.g. "this post needs a title").
- **Forgot your password?** Whoever set it up can change it for you in a
  minute — nothing is lost.
- **Multiple people can help.** You can have an "editor" (can change text and
  posts) or a "writer" (can only add blog posts) with their own passwords.

If anything ever feels stuck, paste the red message to your AI assistant and
ask it to help — that's what it's there for.
